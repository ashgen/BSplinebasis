## 2024-05-18 - Optimize std::vector and std::array initialization
**Learning:** In C++, constructing a `std::vector` of `std::array` elements explicitly with a zeroed-out initial value `std::vector<std::array<T, N>>(size, make_array(0))` is surprisingly much slower than simply relying on default value-initialization `std::vector<std::array<T, N>>(size)`. Default value-initialization often maps to highly optimized `memset` under the hood. For local arrays, `std::array<T, N>{}` (aggregate initialization) is also more efficient.
**Action:** When creating vectors of `std::array` types that need zeroing, just pass the size parameter. Avoid explicitly passing a fully zero-initialized prototype array as a secondary constructor argument, as it prevents optimized continuous zeroing.
